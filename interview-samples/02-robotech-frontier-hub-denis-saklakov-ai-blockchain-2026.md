# "The AI Making the Decision Should Not Control the Record of What It Did" — Denis Saklakov, Founder of RoboTech Frontier Hub, on AI Verification and Blockchain

*Originally published: September 17, 2026*

---

Denis Saklakov, Managing Partner of RoboTech Frontier Hub, spoke about why AI systems need external verification infrastructure, how blockchain can establish trustworthy records of machine decisions, and what his AI-assisted investment tool Meijin does differently from conventional portfolio management software.

**Summary**
- Meijin monitors assets after purchase and manages exit strategies calibrated to the investor's selected risk level rather than predicting which assets to buy.
- Blockchain can record AI system states, permissions, decision conditions, and execution history without requiring the AI itself to run on-chain.
- RoboTech Frontier Hub is developing projects spanning AI verification, robotics, human-machine interfaces, and advanced computing.
- As AI takes a larger role in automated financial decisions, Saklakov expects verification and deterministic execution systems to become a core part of investment infrastructure.

## Tell us about your professional background.

My background crosses law, mathematical statistics, finance, artificial intelligence, and technology. I trained originally as a lawyer before moving into corporate finance, investment banking, M&A, private equity, and asset management. I hold a master's degree and completed the MIT Applied AI Science postgraduate program more recently.

Today I serve as Managing Partner of RoboTech Frontier Hub in New York City. Much of my current work focuses on what I describe as AI and AGI architecture: defining how intelligent systems should use information, make decisions, and interact with the world without allowing computational capability to automatically become uncontrolled authority.

That question has direct practical applications in finance, robotics, AI verification, human-machine interfaces, and advanced computing. I covered the broader architecture problem in my recent book, Before the Machine Chooses for Us.

## How did RoboTech Frontier Hub come together, and what is Meijin?

RoboTech Frontier Hub grew from a problem I encountered repeatedly while working with scientific and engineering teams.

Interesting technology often fails to develop properly because inventors fear that explaining the underlying scientific principle will allow competitors to replicate the product. So the science remains hidden. But when the science is not visible, other researchers cannot test it, criticize it, identify its limits, or connect it to adjacent fields.

We operate differently. We publish enough of the scientific foundation for external researchers to examine and challenge it. We protect the actual implementation, algorithms, and commercial technology. We do not want to hide the science to protect the product. We want the scientific idea to survive examination, and we build on what remains valid.

Meijin reflects that approach.

The core observation is simple. Investors typically devote significant effort to deciding what to buy, but apply much less disciplined thinking to deciding when to sell. Meijin does not select the asset. You choose Bitcoin, Ethereum, a stock, or a commodity contract. Meijin starts working after you own it.

The system first quantifies the level of risk the investor is prepared to accept. It then monitors the position continuously and manages an exit strategy around that risk profile. The goal is to reduce the emotional and cognitive load of the exit decision, which is where most portfolio damage actually happens.

## What role does blockchain play in making AI systems trustworthy for financial use?

The piece of the system that needs to be cryptographically anchored to an independent ledger is not the AI computation itself. It is the state of the system, the permissions granted, the conditions under which a decision was made, and the execution history.

The important principle is that the AI making a decision should not control the record that is later used to verify what happened. A system that can make a decision and then rewrite the history of that decision is not auditable in any meaningful sense.

Blockchain does not make an AI model correct. A false statement can be stored on a blockchain with perfect fidelity. What blockchain provides is provenance and a record that the decision-making system itself cannot easily alter.

For investors, that distinction becomes increasingly significant as AI moves from providing market commentary to systems capable of interacting directly with accounts, exchanges, and financial infrastructure.

Consider a parallel in medical technology. You might want a device to perform a precise operation, but you also need a tamper-proof external record defining exactly what that device is and is not permitted to do. That is precisely the category of problem for which an independently secured cryptographic ledger becomes genuinely useful.

## What happens to financial markets as AI moves faster than human supervision can realistically track?

The practical question for anyone operating in markets is not whether AI development is moving too fast in an abstract sense. It is what happens when increasingly capable AI systems begin analyzing markets, executing strategies, and communicating with financial infrastructure faster than a human being can supervise each individual decision.

That is why I think the next stage of AI in finance is not about better prediction. It is about better verification.

A system that produces a trading recommendation in milliseconds is not particularly useful if no one can subsequently determine whether the underlying information was reliable or whether the system had authority to initiate the resulting transaction.

This is where AI and blockchain infrastructure become complementary rather than separate. AI performs increasingly sophisticated analysis. Cryptographic infrastructure helps establish provenance, permissions, and a verifiable record. Deterministic execution systems define what the AI is actually permitted to do.

For the retail investor, all of that should ultimately become invisible at the user level. The practical experience should be straightforward: you selected an asset, you defined how much risk you are willing to accept, the system monitors it continuously, and you can understand afterward why it acted as it did.

That is the direction Meijin is built toward.
