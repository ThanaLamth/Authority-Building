# "Better Architecture Beats Better Predictions" — David Doss, Founder of CKC Fund, on Institutional Digital Asset Investing

*Originally published: September 24, 2026*

---

In this conversation, David Doss, founder and managing director of CKC Fund, discusses his approach to institutional digital asset investing, how the fund manages risk across volatile markets, and what the regulatory environment means for tokenization going forward.

**Summary**
- Doss says risk management, liquidity discipline, and segregated portfolios matter more than short-term market predictions when operating in digital assets.
- Clearer stablecoin regulation under the GENIUS Act has improved institutional confidence, while the failure of the CLARITY Act cloture vote risks pushing more tokenization activity to offshore markets.
- CKC Fund concentrates primarily on Bitcoin, Ethereum, and highly liquid digital assets, with AI intellectual property investments held in a separate vehicle.
- The 2026 market decline appeared to be an orderly reduction in leverage rather than a systemic failure, and Doss is monitoring global liquidity and Bitcoin resistance levels through the end of the year.

---

## Tell us about your journey to founding CKC Fund.

My career has two distinct phases: roughly a decade in research, education, and technology, followed by another decade in digital assets.

I started in academic research in 2005, including a Fulbright graduate research scholarship, before moving into education technology and growth leadership. That work taught me to follow the evidence and build the operational infrastructure that turns promising ideas into functioning businesses.

In digital assets, I kept running into the same structural gap: capable traders on one side, serious investors on the other, and not enough institutional infrastructure connecting them.

CKC was built to close that gap. Not through better prediction, but through better architecture. Segregated portfolios, non-custodial execution, auditable net asset value, and clear separation between manager and investor assets. Structure first, strategy second. I think that ordering matters.

## How did you develop your interest in digital assets?

The technology drew me in before the price action did. A financial ledger that any participant can independently verify represents a meaningful shift away from systems built entirely around trusted intermediaries.

My interest started around 2016. What was theoretical then is becoming practical now: stablecoin payments, tokenized assets, on-chain proof of holdings. The original promise of the technology is starting to show up in the infrastructure.

## Walk us through CKC Fund's investment philosophy.

In a market this volatile, the durable advantage is risk management, not prediction.

We separate market exposure, momentum strategies, yield approaches, and longer-horizon private investments rather than consolidating them into a single portfolio. Each has a meaningfully different risk profile, and blending them creates exposure that is difficult to measure or manage.

Custody structure matters equally. Traders executing strategies on the fund cannot withdraw investor assets. We size positions for the drawdowns we can realistically withstand, not the returns we hope to achieve.

My background in competitive épée fencing taught me something transferable here: winning consistently is less about moving fastest than about controlling distance and choosing the right moment to act.

## Which digital assets does the fund invest in?

We focus primarily on Bitcoin, Ethereum, and a small number of highly liquid digital assets.

Liquidity comes first. We need to be confident we can exit a position under stressed market conditions without moving the market ourselves. Beyond liquidity, we look for a clear economic purpose and sufficient derivatives-market depth to manage downside risk. If we cannot explain the asset clearly or model the exit, we do not take the position.

## Are you investing in AI and blockchain intellectual property?

Yes, selectively. Defensible intellectual property in AI-enabled media is interesting to us, including patents and equity in the companies developing them.

Those investments sit in a dedicated vehicle rather than being mixed with liquid digital assets. The risk profiles and timelines are completely different, and combining them would obscure what each is actually doing.

As AI models become cheaper and more accessible, lasting value will increasingly concentrate in proprietary data, distribution reach, and enforceable intellectual property.

## Has the GENIUS Act changed the stablecoin investment environment?

The Act has made stablecoins easier to work with by clarifying standards around reserves, audits, and redemptions. That gives both banks and institutional investors greater confidence in the infrastructure.

It has also made the business model more competitive. Because issuers cannot pay interest directly under the framework, more economic value is shifting toward exchanges, wallets, and distribution platforms.

The next major regulatory question involves stablecoin rewards. Banks view them as competition for deposits. Crypto platforms see them as a mechanism for sharing value with users. That tension is not resolved.

## What is your view on the CLARITY Act failing its cloture vote?

The CLARITY Act would have created a pathway for digital assets to move from securities treatment to commodity treatment as their networks decentralize. The industry benefits from rules it can follow in advance rather than discovering its boundaries through enforcement actions.

If the Act does not pass, tokenization activity will not stop. A larger share of it will simply move offshore or remain in private markets where it is less visible and less accessible to ordinary investors.

The United States risks losing market share, high-value jobs, and influence over the standards that will shape this asset class globally.

## What is your market outlook for the rest of 2026?

I do not give short-term price targets. I focus on the conditions that drive market behavior.

The 2026 decline looked like an orderly reduction in leverage rather than a breakdown in underlying infrastructure. Exchanges continued operating, stablecoin infrastructure held up, and no major intermediary failed. That represents real progress relative to previous market cycles.

Through the remainder of the year, I am watching global liquidity conditions, how quickly leverage returns to the system, and whether Bitcoin can break through the resistance levels that have defined the range in recent months. A gradual recovery would be healthier than another fast, heavily leveraged rally.

## What would you like investors to keep in mind?

Ask every manager a direct question: who verified the numbers, and when?

A reported return, valuation, or track record is only as reliable as the process and the verification behind it. The industry has made substantial progress on infrastructure over the past several years. It now needs the same discipline applied to reporting and transparency standards.
