# Crypto Exchange Hack Alert: What to Check After Any Platform Breach

**How to use this brief:** This article is an evergreen verification template. It describes what a confirmed exchange security incident looks like, what an exchange is required to disclose, and what steps affected users should take. Sample data uses a July 2026 hypothetical incident for illustration. Replace bracketed fields with live data when a real incident occurs.

ByBit Exchange disclosed a security incident on July 14, 2026, according to its official statement published on X (formerly Twitter) at 09:42 UTC. $47 million in USDT was confirmed moved from a hot wallet to an unidentified external address, per on-chain data published by Arkham Intelligence at 09:58 UTC.

*[Sample data for illustration. This brief uses a hypothetical ByBit incident dated July 14, 2026. All figures are illustrative.]*

## What ByBit Disclosed About the Incident

ByBit's official statement, published at 09:42 UTC on July 14, confirmed that "unauthorized access to a single hot wallet occurred between 08:10 and 08:47 UTC." The exchange stated that cold wallets were not affected. Withdrawal processing was paused at 09:15 UTC, per the same statement.

The exchange did not disclose the attack vector in its initial statement. A post-mortem timeline was promised within 72 hours, per ByBit's official communications team.

At the time of writing, ByBit had not filed a report with a named financial regulator. Their previous regulatory disclosure obligations depend on jurisdiction of incorporation; this information had not been confirmed by the time of this update.

## Which Funds or Assets Are Confirmed Affected

$47 million in USDT was confirmed moved without authorization, per Arkham Intelligence's on-chain trace published at 09:58 UTC. The receiving address had not received prior transfers from ByBit-labeled addresses, per the same source.

$47 million in funds moved is not the same as $47 million in irrecoverable loss. On-chain funds remain traceable. Whether recovery is possible depends on whether the funds have been bridged, mixed, or converted to another asset. At the time of writing, the funds remained in the original receiving address, per Etherscan data.

No user balances were confirmed as permanently impaired in ByBit's initial statement. The exchange stated that user funds are "fully backed" by reserve assets, which has not been independently verified as of this update.

## Which Users and Jurisdictions Are in Scope

ByBit has not issued a statement identifying which user accounts or jurisdictions are directly affected. Their user base includes registered users in the United Arab Emirates (country of incorporation), the EU under MiCA, and Southeast Asian jurisdictions.

Users in the United States are not permitted to hold accounts on ByBit, per the exchange's terms of service. However, unverified accounts in restricted jurisdictions exist on many platforms; this should not be assumed to limit the affected user population.

## What Remains Unconfirmed or Under Investigation

The attack vector has not been disclosed. The identity of the attacker is unknown. Whether the funds have moved from the original receiving address is subject to change and should be monitored on-chain via Etherscan or Arkham Intelligence.

ByBit's claim that cold wallets are unaffected has not been independently verified by an on-chain security firm as of the time of writing.

No law enforcement agency had issued a public statement on the incident as of the time of this update.

## What Affected Users Should Watch

Users with funds on ByBit should check whether withdrawal processing has resumed. Withdrawal pauses are a common precautionary measure during security reviews and do not confirm that user funds are impaired.

Watch the ByBit official X account and status page (status.bybit.com) for updates. Confirm that any withdrawal you initiate completes on-chain — do not rely solely on the exchange interface to confirm transfers.

If personal account data was part of the breach (not confirmed in this incident), monitor for phishing attempts targeting ByBit users.
