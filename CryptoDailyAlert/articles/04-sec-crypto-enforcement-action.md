# SEC Crypto Enforcement Action: What the Filing Shows and What It Does Not

The SEC filed a civil complaint against NovaCoin Inc. on July 22, 2026, in the US District Court for the Southern District of New York, naming allegations of unregistered securities offering and market manipulation, per the official court filing published at sec.gov on July 22.

## What the SEC Filed or Announced

The filing is a civil complaint, not a criminal indictment. Filing a civil complaint means the SEC is alleging violations of securities law and seeking remedies through a federal court. It does not mean NovaCoin Inc. has been found liable. A court must adjudicate the complaint before any finding of liability.

The complaint names two specific charges: (1) offering and selling unregistered securities in the form of NOVA tokens between January 2024 and March 2026, and (2) engaging in coordinated trading to artificially influence the NOVA token price during the same period.

The SEC's filing quotes the Securities Act of 1933 Section 5 and the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 Section 9(a)(2) as the statutory basis for both counts, per the complaint document published at sec.gov/litigation/complaints/2026/comp-pr2026-NovaCoin.pdf.

## Which Entities Are Named and What the Allegations Are

NovaCoin Inc. is named as the primary defendant. Marcus T. Reyes, listed as CEO of NovaCoin Inc., is named as a co-defendant. The complaint alleges that Reyes directed the token sale and the trading coordination.

"Alleged" means the SEC has presented these claims in a legal filing. Neither NovaCoin Inc. nor Reyes has been found guilty of the alleged conduct. They retain the right to contest all charges.

NovaCoin Inc. issued a statement on July 22 describing the allegations as "without merit" and stating that the company intends to "vigorously defend against all charges," per a statement published on novacoin.io on July 22, 2026.

## Which Actions or Sanctions Are Confirmed vs. Pending Court Approval

The SEC requested: (1) a permanent injunction against NovaCoin Inc. and Reyes from further securities violations, (2) disgorgement of alleged ill-gotten gains with prejudgment interest, and (3) civil monetary penalties.

None of these remedies are in effect. All are requested remedies pending court approval. A federal judge has not issued a ruling on any of the three requested remedies as of the time of writing.

The SEC also requested an emergency asset freeze order. The court granted a temporary restraining order (TRO) freezing NovaCoin Inc. assets on July 22, 2026, per the court docket. A TRO is a temporary measure. A hearing to determine whether a preliminary injunction should replace the TRO is scheduled for August 5, 2026, per the same docket.

## What Remains Unclear or Subject to Legal Process

The complaint does not define how the SEC calculated the alleged ill-gotten gains figure of $84 million. That figure is a claimed amount, not a verified or adjudicated amount.

The jurisdictional scope of the asset freeze has not been publicly detailed. Whether NovaCoin Inc.'s non-US assets are subject to the freeze depends on enforcement mechanisms not specified in the publicly available filing.

NovaCoin Inc.'s token, NOVA, continues to trade on secondary markets. The SEC filing does not halt secondary market trading. Whether exchanges delist or restrict NOVA trading is a decision made by each exchange under its own policies.

## What Affected Parties Should Watch

The August 5, 2026 preliminary injunction hearing will determine whether the TRO asset freeze continues. If converted to a preliminary injunction, the asset freeze remains in place until the case is resolved.

Both parties must submit discovery schedules within 30 days of the initial court appearance, per standard SDNY procedures. Follow the court docket at pacer.gov for filings.

Users holding NOVA tokens should monitor exchange-specific announcements. The SEC filing does not compel any exchange to restrict NOVA trading, but individual exchange compliance teams may act independently.
