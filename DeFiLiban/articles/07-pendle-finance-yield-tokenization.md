# Pendle Finance Yield Tokenization: PT, YT, and the Mechanics Behind Fixed Yield on DeFi

Pendle Finance solves a problem that did not exist before yield-bearing tokens became common: there is no native mechanism in DeFi to separate the principal value of a yield-bearing asset from its future yield. If you hold wstETH (Lido's wrapped staked ETH), you hold both the staked ETH principal and the right to all future staking yield. Pendle splits these into two transferable tokens, creating markets for both independently.

The implication is that yield buyers and principal buyers now have separate, priced instruments. A yield buyer who believes staking APY will rise can acquire YT (Yield Token) to express that view with leveraged exposure. A principal buyer who wants to lock in today's yield at a fixed rate can acquire PT (Principal Token) at a discount to face value and hold it to maturity. The Pendle AMM prices both continuously, creating a market-implied forward yield curve for each underlying asset.

Pendle v2 launched in April 2023. Total value locked peaked at approximately .7 billion in mid-2024 before declining as stETH yield rates compressed through late 2024 into 2025. As of Q2 2026, Pendle remained the largest yield tokenization protocol by TVL.

## What Pendle Does: Splitting Yield From Principal

Pendle's core operation is a wrapping and splitting process:

1. A user deposits a yield-bearing token (wstETH, sDAI, sUSDT, ezETH, or others) into Pendle.
2. Pendle wraps this into an SY (Standardized Yield) token, which normalizes yield accrual accounting across different yield token formats.
3. Pendle splits the SY into two components: PT (Principal Token) and YT (Yield Token), each representing a different claim on the underlying.

PT is redeemable for exactly 1 unit of the underlying SY at maturity. YT entitles the holder to all yield generated by 1 unit of SY from now until maturity. The split is exact: PT + YT = 1 SY, always.

At maturity:
- PT holders redeem 1 PT for 1 SY (or the underlying asset via auto-redemption)
- YT holders receive the accumulated yield from 1 SY over the period; after maturity, YT has no remaining value

## Mechanism Table: SY, PT, and YT Creation, Pricing, and Expiry

| Token | What it represents | How it is created | At maturity | Market price driver |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SY | Normalized yield-bearing token | Wrapping wstETH, sDAI, etc. into Pendle's standard interface | Redeemable for underlying 1:1 | Tracks underlying yield-bearing asset |
| PT | Principal claim: redeemable for 1 SY at maturity | Split from SY; sold on Pendle AMM | Redeemable for 1 SY (= 1 underlying + yield accrued during hold) | Trades at a discount; discount = fixed implied yield |
| YT | Yield claim: all yield from 1 SY until maturity | Split from SY; sold on Pendle AMM | Expires worthless (yield has been distributed) | Trades at speculative yield expectation; decays to 0 at expiry |

## How PT Gives Fixed Yield and What the Discount Represents

PT trades at a discount to its maturity value of 1 SY. The discount is the fixed yield. If PT for wstETH with a three-month maturity trades at .97 per PT (where 1 SY wstETH = .00 at face value), then buying 1 PT for .97 and holding to maturity returns .00 -- a fixed gain of ~3% over three months, or approximately 12.4% annualized.

The formal pricing:

`
PT price = 1 / (1 + implied_yield * time_to_maturity_in_years)
`

This is a discount pricing model equivalent to zero-coupon bonds in traditional finance. The PT buyer is locking in today's market-implied yield at a fixed rate regardless of what happens to the underlying staking yield between now and maturity. If stETH staking APY drops from 4% to 2% tomorrow, PT buyers who locked in at 4% implied yield still receive 4% for the remaining period.

The risk to PT holders: the price of 1 SY at maturity. PT redeems for 1 SY, and SY represents 1 unit of the underlying yield-bearing asset (e.g., wstETH). If wstETH price falls 30% between PT purchase and maturity, the PT holder receives 1 wstETH equivalent at a price 30% lower than the purchase price of the PT. PT does not provide principal protection against the underlying asset's price movement -- it provides fixed yield rate protection against variable underlying yield.

## How YT Gives Leveraged Yield Exposure and What the Risk Is

YT is the counterpart to PT: it captures all yield from 1 SY over the remaining period and expires worthless at maturity.

YT pricing is the market's estimate of total future yield from 1 SY until maturity:

`
YT price ≈ underlying_yield_rate * time_to_maturity * SY_price
`

(Simplified; the Pendle AMM uses a more precise formula based on the implied yield curve.)

## How the Pendle AMM Prices PT and YT

Pendle uses a custom AMM called the Pendle AMM (V2 architecture) that is designed specifically for PT/YT pricing, not a general constant-product invariant.

The Pendle AMM has one pool per PT/SY pair. The pool contains PT and SY, and uses a modified StableSwap-like invariant that accounts for time to maturity: as maturity approaches, the implied yield from a PT/SY trade approaches zero (PT approaches 1:1 parity with SY). This means the AMM's effective curve shifts continuously toward a 1:1 exchange rate as time passes, even without any trades.

YT does not have a direct AMM pool. YT price is derived from the PT/SY pool: since PT + YT = 1 SY, YT_price = SY_price - PT_price. Buying YT on Pendle's interface actually executes a combination of SY purchase and PT flash-sale, not a direct YT purchase from a liquidity pool.

The Pendle AMM's modification to handle time decay means that LPs in the PT/SY pool face a different impermanent loss profile than standard AMM LPs: IL in Pendle's pool is influenced by implied yield rate changes rather than raw token price changes. The AMM is designed to reduce LP IL from time-decay-driven yield convergence, but yield rate volatility (changes in the underlying protocol's APY) still creates LP risk.

## Risk Profile: Smart Contract, Liquidity, Oracle, and Maturity Risk

### Smart contract risk

Pendle V2 was audited by Ackee Blockchain Security and conducted internal security reviews before its April 2023 launch. Additional audits were completed by multiple firms through 2023-2024 as new asset integrations were added. As of July 2026, Pendle V2 has not experienced a critical exploit. The audit list and reports are available at the Pendle Finance GitHub and pendle.finance/security. Each new SY wrapper for a new underlying asset (e.g., wrapping Ethena's sUSDe) requires a separate integration audit, as the SY layer is the point where underlying protocol risk enters Pendle.

### Maturity risk

PT and YT are time-bounded instruments. At maturity:
- PT must be actively redeemed, or it will expire unredeemed in the user's wallet. Unredeemed PT does not lose value -- it can be redeemed after maturity at any time -- but it earns no yield post-maturity.
- YT expires to zero value after maturity. A YT holder who does not claim accumulated yield before maturity (or whose yield was continuously distributed) will receive nothing post-maturity.

For non-technical users, the maturity mechanic creates a UI risk: holding YT past maturity thinking it still has value is an error Pendle's interface attempts to mitigate with maturity countdown displays, but the underlying risk remains.

### Liquidity risk

Pendle's PT/SY AMM pools are significantly thinner than the underlying asset markets (wstETH, sDAI, etc.). Large PT or YT trades incur substantial slippage relative to the position size. Pendle's interface shows price impact for each trade, but the implicit assumption that a large YT position can be exited at near-market price is often incorrect for positions above a few hundred thousand dollars in the thinner maturities.

### Oracle risk

Pendle does not use external price oracles for PT/YT pricing -- the AMM is the price. But the underlying SY assets depend on external oracles or on-chain redemption rates: wstETH's exchange rate with ETH, sDAI's exchange rate with DAI, and others. If an underlying asset's yield mechanism is compromised (e.g., a stETH depeg), the SY price used to settle Pendle positions is affected. This is inherited risk from the base protocol, not Pendle-specific oracle risk.

## Comparable Protocols: Element Finance and Sense Protocol

**Element Finance** operated a similar PT/YT split model. It ran on Ethereum from 2021, partnered with Balancer for liquidity, and ceased active operations in July 2023 after the team announced they were pivoting to a new product. Element's architecture was not exploited; the protocol wound down intentionally. Its closure illustrates liquidity protocol risk that is not exploit-based: market adoption and team continuity are protocol-survival prerequisites.

**Sense Protocol** offers fixed-rate DeFi through a similar principal/yield separation model on Ethereum mainnet. Sense's TVL has remained substantially smaller than Pendle's throughout 2023-2026. The architecture is comparable in concept; the practical difference for users is liquidity depth -- Sense markets are thinner and trade at wider spreads than equivalent Pendle markets.

## Yield and Risk Trade-Off: Fixed vs. Variable Yield Positions in Pendle

**PT buyers** lock in a fixed annualized yield at today's implied rate. They accept:
- No yield rate upside: if the underlying yield rate rises after PT purchase, PT buyers do not benefit
- Price exposure to the underlying asset: PT redeems for 1 SY, not for a USD value; if wstETH falls, the PT's USD value at maturity falls proportionally
- Liquidity risk: exiting PT before maturity requires AMM slippage

**YT buyers** take leveraged yield exposure. They accept:
- Theta-like decay: YT value erodes toward zero continuously as maturity approaches and yield is distributed
- Yield rate sensitivity: a yield rate drop below the implied yield at purchase can make YT worthless before maturity
- Full capital loss at maturity: YT expires at zero value; capital is returned only through yield accrual during the holding period

**PT/SY LPs** earn AMM trading fees from PT/YT trades. They accept:
- LP IL from implied yield rate changes (not raw price changes)
- Smart contract risk from both Pendle AMM and the underlying SY protocol
- Liquidity illiquidity: PT/SY LP positions have fixed maturity; the pool expires with the PT maturity

The YT risk profile deserves special emphasis: YT is one of the most mechanically misunderstood instruments in DeFi. It provides leveraged yield exposure, not leveraged asset exposure. The underlying asset (wstETH, sDAI) could stay flat or increase in value while YT still expires worthless if the yield rate collapses. Understanding the difference between asset price risk and yield rate risk is the prerequisite for trading YT without significant expected capital loss.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Pendle Finance?**
Pendle Finance is a DeFi protocol that splits yield-bearing tokens (like wstETH, sDAI, or sUSDT) into two separate tokens: PT (Principal Token), which redeems for the underlying at a fixed future price, and YT (Yield Token), which captures all yield generated by the underlying until maturity. This allows users to either lock in a fixed yield rate (by buying PT at a discount) or gain leveraged exposure to future yield changes (by buying YT).

**What is a Principal Token (PT) in Pendle?**
PT is a token that redeems for exactly 1 unit of the underlying SY token at a fixed maturity date. It trades at a discount to its face value, and the discount represents the fixed implied yield. A PT trading at .97 for a 1 ETH face value at three-month maturity implies approximately 12.4% annualized fixed yield. PT holders are protected against yield rate changes but not against changes in the underlying asset's price.

**What is a Yield Token (YT) in Pendle?**
YT entitles the holder to all yield generated by 1 unit of the underlying SY token from now until maturity. YT provides leveraged exposure to the underlying yield rate: small changes in the yield rate produce larger percentage changes in YT value. YT expires worthless at maturity -- the yield has been distributed during the holding period, and the token itself has no residual value. A yield rate collapse before maturity can drive YT value to near zero.

**Does Pendle have impermanent loss?**
Yes, but differently from standard AMMs. Pendle's PT/SY AMM pools have IL that is primarily driven by changes in the implied yield rate rather than raw token price changes. The AMM's curve accounts for time-to-maturity, so normal time decay produces convergence toward 1:1 PT/SY pricing without LP loss from convergence alone. The LP risk is yield rate volatility (the underlying protocol's APY changing) rather than price ratio volatility.

**What happened to Element Finance?**
Element Finance ran a similar yield tokenization model on Ethereum from 2021. It ceased active operations in July 2023 when the team announced they were pivoting to a new product. The protocol was not exploited; it wound down intentionally. Most Element liquidity migrated to Pendle, which had launched in April 2023. Element's closure demonstrates that yield tokenization protocol risk includes team and adoption continuity, not only smart contract or market risks.

**Can YT go to zero before maturity?**
Yes. YT value is approximately equal to (expected future yield) * (time remaining). If the underlying protocol's yield rate drops significantly before maturity, YT value falls proportionally. If the yield rate approaches zero, YT approaches zero in value even with weeks or months remaining before maturity. The capital invested in YT is only recovered through yield accrual during the holding period -- if yield collapses, that recovery mechanism weakens accordingly.
