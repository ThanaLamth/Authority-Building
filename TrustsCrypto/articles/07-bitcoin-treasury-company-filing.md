Strategy Inc. (the company formerly known as MicroStrategy) disclosed its Bitcoin holdings, financing structure, and related risk factors in a series of SEC filings beginning with its Q3 2020 10-Q and continuing quarterly through the present. The most recent holdings figure disclosed before this article's review date was contained in the company's Form 8-K filed with the SEC on EDGAR. All holdings figures cited below are sourced from SEC filings and should be verified against the current EDGAR record before relying on them for investment decisions.

## What Strategy's Filing Disclosed About Its Bitcoin Holdings

Strategy began disclosing Bitcoin holdings through Form 8-K filings that supplement its quarterly and annual reports. The company adopted a practice of issuing an 8-K each time it makes a material Bitcoin purchase, disclosing the amount purchased, the aggregate purchase price, and the updated total holdings.

As of the most recent 10-Q before this article's review, Strategy held in excess of 400,000 BTC. The exact figure, average purchase price per BTC, and aggregate carrying value as of the filing date are disclosed in the specific EDGAR filing and are subject to change with each subsequent purchase announcement.

The company's balance sheet treatment of Bitcoin changed in 2025 following the Financial Accounting Standards Board's adoption of ASU 2023-08, which requires entities that hold crypto assets to measure them at fair value through earnings each reporting period. Prior to this standard, Strategy carried its Bitcoin at cost less impairment. After adoption, quarterly mark-to-market changes in Bitcoin price flow directly through its income statement.

## Why the Structure and Financing Method Matter for Shareholders

Strategy has financed its Bitcoin purchases through multiple mechanisms. These include at-the-money equity offerings, where the company sells new common stock and uses proceeds to purchase Bitcoin; convertible senior notes, which are debt instruments that holders can convert into equity; and preferred stock offerings. Each mechanism dilutes existing shareholders in different ways and carries different risk characteristics.

The convertible notes carry interest rates stated in the individual note prospectus and mature at dates disclosed in those filings. If Bitcoin's price declines materially, the company's debt obligations remain fixed while the market value of its collateral falls. The note terms do not include covenants that automatically require the company to liquidate Bitcoin if the price falls below a threshold, but the refinancing risk at maturity depends on market conditions at that time.

The risk factors section of Strategy's most recent annual report (Form 10-K) states in its own language that the company's bitcoin holdings are subject to extreme volatility, that a significant decline in Bitcoin's price could adversely affect the company's financial condition and results of operations, and that the company may not be able to pay its debts if Bitcoin prices fall significantly. Quoting from the filing is more informative than paraphrasing: the company's own risk factor language specifies the scenarios it considers material.

## Which Shareholders and Counterparties Are Directly Affected

Common shareholders are directly affected by the equity dilution from at-the-money offerings. Each offering is disclosed via a prospectus supplement filed on EDGAR. The total number of shares issued and the proceeds used to purchase Bitcoin are reported in subsequent quarterly filings.

Holders of Strategy's convertible notes hold debt against a company whose primary asset is Bitcoin. Their credit exposure depends on Bitcoin's price, the company's ability to refinance or repay at maturity, and the conversion premium relative to the current stock price.

Strategy's Bitcoin custodian has not been publicly disclosed in full detail in its SEC filings. The 10-K risk factors note custody risk as a factor but do not name the custodian with specificity in the public filings reviewed for this article. Pending confirmation: the identity and terms of Strategy's custody arrangement were not fully disclosed in the SEC filings reviewed at time of writing.

## What Has Not Been Disclosed or Remains Subject to Future Filings

The specific cold storage configuration and multi-signature arrangement for Strategy's Bitcoin have not been disclosed in public SEC filings. The company's stated policy is to hold Bitcoin on behalf of its treasury, but the technical custody structure -- whether it uses third-party qualified custodians, self-custody, or a combination -- was not fully described in the EDGAR filings reviewed.

An independent auditor signs Strategy's financial statements but audits financial assertions, not Bitcoin holdings directly. The company's Bitcoin count disclosed in its filings has not been independently verified through a proof-of-reserves mechanism that is public at time of writing. That is a structural difference from exchange-published proof-of-reserves, which are independently attested on-chain.

## What Investors and Analysts Should Watch After This Filing

Strategy files a new 8-K each time it makes a Bitcoin purchase. Those filings are the most current disclosure of its holdings and are the source that should be consulted before citing a specific holdings figure, because the total changes frequently.

The company's ability to refinance its convertible notes at maturity is the primary credit risk to monitor. Maturity dates for each series of notes are disclosed in the original note prospectus and in the debt schedule in the annual 10-K. If Bitcoin's price at the time of maturity is significantly below the company's average acquisition cost, the refinancing environment will be more constrained.

FASB ASU 2023-08 adoption means Strategy's quarterly earnings will now reflect unrealized gains and losses on Bitcoin directly. Analysts and shareholders should distinguish operating earnings from fair value adjustments when reviewing Strategy's reported net income under the new standard.
