The SEC approved eleven spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds on January 10, 2024, per the agency's order published at sec.gov under Release No. 34-99306. The approval authorized both cash-create and cash-redemption models. Each fund's specific structure -- including custody arrangement, authorized participant list, and fee schedule -- was disclosed in its individual prospectus filed with the SEC on EDGAR.

## What the SEC's Approval Actually Authorized

The January 10 approval covered applications from BlackRock (iShares Bitcoin Trust, ticker IBIT), Fidelity (Wise Origin Bitcoin Fund, FBTC), Invesco Galaxy (BTCO), ARK 21Shares (ARKB), Bitwise (BITB), VanEck (HODL), WisdomTree (BTCW), Valkyrie (BRRR), Franklin Templeton (EZBC), Grayscale (GBTC, converting from its existing Bitcoin Trust), and Hashdex (DEFI, converted from futures).

The approval authorized the funds to hold spot Bitcoin directly, not Bitcoin futures. That distinction matters because a futures-based ETF does not give the fund direct exposure to Bitcoin prices in the same way; it creates exposure through rolling futures contracts, which introduces basis risk that spot funds avoid.

"Approved" means the SEC authorized the funds to begin trading on national securities exchanges. It does not mean the SEC has assessed Bitcoin as an investment, endorsed the funds as suitable for any investor, or waived the funds' ongoing regulatory obligations under the Investment Company Act and Exchange Act.

The approval required all eleven funds to use cash-create and cash-redemption models at launch. Under that structure, authorized participants deliver cash to create new fund shares, not Bitcoin directly. The fund then purchases Bitcoin with that cash. The SEC's rationale was to prevent broker-dealers from holding Bitcoin on their own balance sheets to facilitate in-kind creation.

## Why the Custody and Redemption Structure Matters for Institutional Buyers

Authorized participants are the only entities that can create or redeem fund shares in large blocks directly with the fund. Retail investors buy and sell shares on the secondary market. The authorized participant list for most of the eleven funds named institutional trading firms including Virtu Financial, Jane Street Capital, and ABN AMRO Clearing, among others disclosed per fund in respective prospectus filings.

Custody is held separately from the fund administrator. BlackRock's IBIT, Bitwise's BITB, Franklin Templeton's EZBC, Invesco Galaxy's BTCO, and ARK 21Shares' ARKB disclosed Coinbase Custody Trust Company as their custodian. Fidelity Digital Assets serves as custodian for FBTC. VanEck's HODL uses Gemini Trust Company.

Custodian counterparty risk is a structural consideration not fully disclosed in the SEC's approval order itself. Each fund's prospectus discloses the specific custody agreement terms. The SEC's approval did not standardize custody requirements across the eleven funds; each fund's prospectus governs its arrangement separately.

Management fees at launch ranged from 0.12% annually (BlackRock's promotional rate for the first year) to 1.50% annually (Grayscale GBTC). Fidelity FBTC launched at 0.25%, Franklin EZBC at 0.19%, and Bitwise BITB at 0.20% with a six-month promotional waiver.

## Which Issuers and Products Are Covered by the Approval

The eleven approved funds all hold spot Bitcoin directly. None of the eleven is a futures-based product. That differentiates them from the ProShares Bitcoin Strategy ETF (BITO), which had been trading since October 2021 under a separate approval pathway.

Grayscale's GBTC converted from a non-exchange-traded trust into an ETF under the same January 10 order. Its management fee of 1.50% was significantly higher than the new entrants, which contributed to substantial outflows from GBTC into lower-cost alternatives in the weeks following approval.

IBIT reached USD 10 billion in assets under management in 49 calendar days after launch, which according to Bloomberg data was the fastest ETF of any kind to cross that threshold in U.S. market history.

## What Remains Subject to Further SEC Review or Has Not Been Disclosed

The cash-create requirement was a condition of the January 2024 approval, not a permanent structural feature. Several fund sponsors submitted requests to the SEC to permit in-kind creation and redemption, which would allow authorized participants to deliver Bitcoin directly to create shares. As of this article's last review date, the SEC had not issued a rule change permitting in-kind creates for these funds. Pending confirmation: the status of in-kind creation approval had not been announced at time of writing.

Custodian counterparty risk disclosures vary by fund and are not standardized. If a custodian faces separate regulatory action or an operational failure, each fund's prospectus governs what happens to the held Bitcoin. The SEC's approval order does not guarantee any specific recovery mechanism for fund investors if the custodian faces an adverse event.

The SEC approved spot Ethereum ETFs separately in May 2024. Those products have a distinct approval history, separate custody arrangements, and different staking treatment questions that were not resolved in the Bitcoin ETF approval order.

## What Institutional Buyers and Retail Investors Should Watch

The in-kind redemption question is the most significant structural issue still open. If the SEC permits in-kind creates, the arbitrage mechanism between the fund's share price and Bitcoin's spot price would become more efficient, which typically narrows premiums and discounts. Institutional buyers with an interest in tighter tracking should monitor SEC rulemaking in this area.

Each fund's annual report discloses actual custody costs, realized management fees, and any operational events during the year. The first annual reports for most of these funds were published in early 2025. Investors who want to compare actual custody performance, not just fee rates, should read those filings rather than relying on launch-day comparisons.

Fee competition among the eleven funds has continued since launch. Several funds adjusted their fee schedules after initial promotional periods ended. The most current fee for any specific fund is disclosed in its current prospectus on EDGAR, not in the January 2024 approval order.
