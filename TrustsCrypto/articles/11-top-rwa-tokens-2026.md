### Ondo Finance (ONDO)

Ondo Finance is the most institutionally integrated RWA project on-chain, with OUSG and USDY holding a combined AUM exceeding $700 million as of July 2026, according to Ondo's public transparency page.

OUSG is backed by BlackRock's BUIDL fund, which holds short-duration US Treasuries and is listed on the SEC's EDGAR database. That makes OUSG one of the few tokenized products where the underlying collateral can be independently verified through a public regulatory filing, not just a protocol dashboard.

What stands out from reviewing Ondo's public documentation is how carefully the team separates institutional access products from retail-facing ones. USDY is available to non-US persons with KYC. OUSG is gated to qualified purchasers. That distinction matters for risk profiling.

Investors who worked through ONDO's onboarding directly [found the qualified purchaser gate](https://www.reddit.com/r/CryptoCurrency/comments/1b9wg4h/) to be the primary friction point, not the product itself. The ONDO governance token does not grant direct yield from fund products.

**Best for:**
- Institutional allocators seeking on-chain US Treasury exposure with SEC-traceable collateral
- DeFi protocols integrating yield-bearing stablecoin alternatives (Flux Finance, Morpho, Aave Arc)
- Researchers comparing tokenized fund structures by regulatory depth

**Tradeoffs:**
- Retail access to OUSG is blocked by qualified purchaser gating
- ONDO token price is driven by governance and narrative, not direct fund yield
- Multi-chain presence across Ethereum and Solana creates reconciliation complexity for institutional users

### Centrifuge (CFG)

Centrifuge is the most established private credit tokenization protocol, with over $600 million in financed assets across more than 1,500 individual loans as of Q2 2026, according to Centrifuge's public pool data.

The protocol structures each loan pool as a separate legal entity, typically a Cayman SPV or a US LLC, with senior and junior tranches issued on-chain. MakerDAO has used Centrifuge pools as collateral for DAI since 2021, providing a documented track record of on-chain credit integration.

From reviewing Centrifuge's live pool interface, what stands out is the tranche disclosure model. Each pool lists the pool manager, NAV calculation methodology, and outstanding debt by borrower bucket. That granularity is uncommon among RWA projects.

The weakness is credit risk. Private credit defaults run through off-chain legal enforcement, which introduces jurisdiction risk not captured in the on-chain NAV.

**Best for:**
- DeFi protocols seeking diversified collateral beyond government bonds
- Researchers tracking the gap between on-chain NAV and realized recovery rates
- Allocators tolerating illiquidity in exchange for higher yield

**Tradeoffs:**
- Liquidity is pool-specific and often locked until maturity
- Borrower default risk is not visible in real time without off-chain legal monitoring
- CFG governance token has limited direct claim on protocol cash flows

### Polymesh (POLYX)

Polymesh is a purpose-built permissioned blockchain for regulated securities, designed by the Polymath team with FCA-adjacent compliance tooling built into the base layer.

The chain enforces identity verification and compliance at the protocol layer. Asset issuers cannot transfer tokens to unverified addresses. That makes it attractive to regulated financial institutions that cannot use permissionless chains.

From reviewing the Polymesh technical documentation, the architecture difference from Ethereum-based RWA projects is significant. On Polymesh, compliance rules are enforced by the settlement engine itself, not by smart contract wrappers that can be upgraded or bypassed. That is a security model advantage for regulated issuers.

The weakness is ecosystem depth. POLYX has fewer DeFi integrations than Ethereum-native tokens because its permissioned architecture is incompatible with open permissionless protocols.

**Best for:**
- Regulated broker-dealers who need compliance-at-layer-1
- Tokenization of traditional securities under existing regulatory frameworks
- Enterprise clients who cannot use permissionless blockchains for legal reasons

**Tradeoffs:**
- Limited DeFi composability by design
- Lower retail trading liquidity than Ethereum-native RWA tokens
- Adoption depends on regulatory approval per jurisdiction, which varies significantly

### Clearpool (CPOOL)

Clearpool is an undercollateralized institutional lending protocol that allows whitelisted institutional borrowers to raise capital directly from DeFi liquidity providers.

Borrowers on Clearpool have passed KYB checks and credit underwriting by the Clearpool team, according to the protocol's public borrower registry. The protocol publishes utilization rates, borrower names, and outstanding balances in real time.

That transparency step distinguishes Clearpool from most comparable protocols, which only disclose aggregate TVL. Knowing who is borrowing matters for any lender evaluating counterparty risk.

The weakness is structural: undercollateralized lending has no on-chain collateral to seize. When a borrower fails to repay, recovery depends entirely on off-chain legal recourse.

**Best for:**
- Yield seekers comfortable with institutional credit risk in exchange for higher APY
- Researchers benchmarking undercollateralized versus overcollateralized lending risk
- DeFi practitioners tracking the institutional credit segment of on-chain lending

**Tradeoffs:**
- No on-chain collateral backing loans; default recovery is entirely off-chain
- Pool yield fluctuates with utilization, which can compress significantly during low-demand periods
- CPOOL governance token does not guarantee a share of protocol revenue

### Realio Network (RIO)

Realio Network focuses on real estate and commodity tokenization, primarily targeting the accredited investor market in the United States under Regulation D exemptions.

RIO is the earliest-stage project in this list by on-chain TVL, with AUM under $100 million as of July 2026, according to Realio's public platform data. The project operates across Algorand and Ethereum, with Algorand-side activity lower by on-chain volume.

From reviewing Realio's public documentation, the Regulation D structure limits investor eligibility to accredited investors. That gating reduces the addressable market significantly compared to Ondo's USDY, which is available to non-US persons globally.

Property-level collateral data is not publicly disclosed in a form that allows independent audit, which is a meaningful transparency gap relative to Centrifuge's loan-level data.

**Best for:**
- Accredited investors seeking direct on-chain real estate exposure
- Researchers tracking early-stage commodity tokenization infrastructure
- Projects evaluating Algorand's role in RWA issuance

**Tradeoffs:**
- Accredited investor gating eliminates most retail participation
- Lower TVL and liquidity than all other projects in this list
- Property-level collateral disclosure is limited compared to Centrifuge's pool data

## Frequently asked questions

**What is an RWA token?**
An RWA token is a blockchain-based representation of an off-chain financial asset, such as a government bond, private loan, real estate deed, or commodity. The on-chain token grants a legal or economic claim on the underlying asset through a structure defined in the project's legal documentation.

**How do I verify the collateral behind an RWA token?**
For US Treasury-backed products like ONDO's OUSG, the underlying fund is visible on the SEC's EDGAR system. For private credit products like Centrifuge, each pool publishes its NAV and borrower buckets on-chain. For other products, look for audited attestations or legal agreements with identifiable counterparties, not just protocol dashboard figures.

**Is the RWA sector regulated?**
Regulation varies by product and jurisdiction. Products structured as registered securities (OUSG) are regulated in the US. Products using offshore SPVs operate under the laws of their domicile. Investors should verify the regulatory status of any product before participating.

**What is the difference between RWA yield and DeFi yield?**
RWA yield comes from off-chain economic activity (loan interest, Treasury coupon, rent) and is brought on-chain through legal agreements. DeFi yield typically comes from protocol emissions, trading fees, or leverage recycling. RWA yield is generally more stable but depends on legal enforcement for recovery in a default scenario.

**Can retail investors access RWA tokens?**
Access depends on the product. ONDO's USDY is available to non-US persons with KYC. Most other products in this list require accredited investor status, qualified purchaser status, or institutional whitelisting. Check each protocol's eligibility documentation directly before attempting to invest.
